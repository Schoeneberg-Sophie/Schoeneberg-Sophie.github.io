About Me

Hello! I'm Sophie Schöneberg, a Data Science Master's student at the University of Mannheim with a strong focus on corporate security management and data science.

Background & Focus

I completed my Bachelor's degree in Political Science at the University of Mannheim, where I focused on International Relations and Statistics with excellent grades. My work centered on large-scale data projects, such as analysing sentiment differences in independent and state-controlled news articles, where I examined over 20,000 articles and their commentary across various economic, social, and political events. I primarily work with R and Python to develop the projects available on my page. I wrote my Bachelor's Thesis on the growing impact of dual-use satellites in space and the dangers that come with blurring the lines between civil and military objects.

I also work as a Student Researcher at the Chair of International Relations, where I collect information on pro-government militias, paramilitaries, and private security companies for various countries, with a primary focus on Africa and Asia. If you are interested in learning more about me, feel free to download my resume or message me on your preferred communication channel.

Interests

My interests lie in both qualitative and quantitative analyses of security-related events, specifically bridging the gap between political science and advanced data science. With my skill set, I aim to drive data-driven decision-making in the fields of Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI), Open Source Intelligence (OSINT), and Geopolitical Risk Modeling within corporate security and supply chain environments. Backed by my academic background and technical expertise, I am motivated by contemporary global disruptions and their impacts on critical infrastructure, logistics, and international business. Here are a few questions I find compelling:

01 // How can we automatically detect and track supply chain vulnerabilities or maritime/aviation disruptions using open-source data and anomaly detection? When a single cargo ship blocks a global trade hub, what mathematical structure tells us in advance where the next fragile bottleneck in the global supply chain will break?

02 // Can Natural Language Processing (NLP) map and forecast geopolitical tensions and state-sponsored disinformation campaigns in real-time?

03 // How do we transition from reactive corporate security to predictive threat intelligence by fusing hard data streams with qualitative geopolitical analysis?

04 // How can we use nocturnal satellite imagery and light anomalies to predict actual economic activity or trouble spots in lockdown regions where all official statistics are manipulated?
