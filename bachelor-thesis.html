Bachelor Thesis: Security Dilemma in Space: Quantitative Analysis of Worldwide Procurement of Dual-Use Satellites

University of Mannheim — Department of Political Science - Faculty of International Relations

Overview

Key Implications & Contributions

1. The Vanishing Civil-Military Binary: The rapid rise of Civil-Military Fusion (CMF) blurs the line between commercial routine and military positioning, turning space into a legally ambiguous "grey zone."

2. Targeting & Legal Risks: Because commercial infrastructure can double as military intelligence, civilian assets face a heightened risk of being perceived or targeted as legitimate military objectives, complicating international law and global security.

Core Research Question & Motivation

Does the proliferation of dual-use satellites indicate a security dilemma in space?

While qualitative research and theoretical implications have long investigated the security dilemma in space, this study addresses a major gap by utilising the first quantitative panel data regression of global space assets from 2000 to 2024 to test whether these theoretical considerations hold up in practice.

Space has increasingly transformed from a state-monopoly commercial commodity into an environment for state actors to defend national sovereignty. Driven by the New Space paradigm and Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs), such as SpaceX mega-constellations, the civil-military binary in orbit is vanishing in favour of integrated space technology. This introduces severe legal ambiguity regarding whether civilian space assets have become legitimate military targets.

Key Findings & Empirical Takeaways

Geopolitical Catalysts & Institutional Momentum: Events like the 2014 Crimean invasion acted as an exogenous shock and catalyst for initial U.S. procurement (such as phasing out Russian RD-180 engines and certifying SpaceX's Falcon 9 in 2015). However, long-term capabilities are primarily driven by high institutional momentum rather than day-to-day reactionary predictions of rival actions.

Decoupling from Traditional Indicators: Total strategic space assets display a functional decoupling from traditional domestic metrics; national military expenditure and regime type are largely insignificant predictors, highlighting the dominant role of commercial companies.

The Action-Reaction Spiral: Western countries, especially the U.S., invested significantly in dual-use technology post-2015, signaling a new era of space security. In response, rival countries like China and Russia have significantly increased their respective Civil-Military Fusion (CMF) capabilities to maintain parity and close the resilience gap.
